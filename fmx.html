01 / Business problem

Reliable data is the foundation of a successful implementation.

A school district may have asset information, building records and maintenance needs distributed across incompatible files. Missing identifiers, duplicate records and inconsistent names make it difficult to migrate information into a facilities management system or make dependable decisions.

The objective

Prepare consistent, validated staging datasets, retain exceptions for review, and translate equipment condition and repair costs into a practical maintenance overview.

Why it matters

Reliable data helps facilities teams focus limited time and funding on the right work. In a real school environment, dependable maintenance supports fewer disruptions and safer, more reliable spaces for students and staff.

The intended use is a practice migration workflow, not an actual upload or assessment of school safety.

02 / Methodology

A repeatable intake-to-reporting workflow

01 · INTAKE

Extract

Import school location and improvement CSV data; expand nested equipment and assessment fields from JSON.

02 · TRANSFORM

Clean & map

Standardize text, identifiers and data types. Map school names and link records using school IDs.

03 · VALIDATE

Reconcile

Check required fields, rating ranges, costs, duplicates and join counts. Separate unresolved rows.

04 · DELIVER

Report

Create staging outputs, PivotTables, dashboards, Google Sheets analysis and a repeatable SOP.

Source inventory

Public school location data, school-level facility improvement CSVs and 13 simulated equipment JSON records.

Integration key

School ID connects facility improvement and equipment records to the prepared school inventory. The equipment merge retains all nine validated records.

Validation rules

Required serial numbers and valid school IDs; condition ratings between 1 and 5; nonnegative repair costs; removal of verified exact duplicates; reconciliation before release.

Exception handling

Keep failed records and their reasons visible, correct only against supporting evidence, and refresh QA before operational use.

03 / Data quality

Account for every record.

The migration simulation uses separate accepted and exception outputs instead of silently dropping questionable records.

05 / Automation & verification

Use AI to accelerate work, not replace quality controls.

What was verified

All seven analytical values in the saved Copilot workbook match the manual workbook. Its QA summary reports 14 of 15 checks passed.

What remains

The remaining saved QA failure concerns eight column headers that do not follow lowercase naming conventions. Recheck after standardization; verify refresh behavior and new-record handling before operational use.

The experiment starts with cleaned data. It does not establish fully automated raw-source migration or production readiness.

06 / Application to customer implementation

How this translates to facilities software

FMX describes customer data migration as part of implementation, including building and equipment information. Its professional services data roles emphasize preparing field data, mapping to a target schema, quality checks and spreadsheet/AI tools. This independent project practices those same kinds of responsibilities without claiming an actual FMX integration.

The takeaway: Data migration is not just transferring information. It is creating a trustworthy foundation for the customer to make decisions from day one.

Project deliverables

Manual Excel integration workbook, Copilot comparison workbook, Google Sheets analysis, original CSV and JSON sources, and a standard operating procedure. This public case study presents aggregate results rather than publishing local workbook paths or simulated school-level assessments.
