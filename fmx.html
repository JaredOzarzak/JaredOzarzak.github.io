How I Cleaned Up School Facilities Data

I matched 227 of 236 improvement records to a school. The other 9 went on a list to check. I also gave Copilot the cleaned equipment data to see if it got the same numbers I did. It got 7 out of 7.
Why I did this

The files didn't always agree

I'm from Columbus, so I used Columbus City Schools data to see what it takes to get these files ready for a system like FMX. I needed to know which school each record belonged to and which rows needed another look. That's the stuff a facilities team needs to get right before it can use the data to plan repairs around the school day.

The data

What I worked with

  • 116 school records from public Columbus City Schools location data
  • 236 facility improvement records from school-level CSV files
  • 13 simulated equipment records in a nested JSON file

This was an independent practice project. The equipment conditions and repair costs are simulated, and I didn't upload anything to FMX.

How I did it

How I put the files together

  1. I loaded the CSVs into Power Query and expanded the equipment and assessment fields in the JSON.
  2. I cleaned up names, IDs, and data types, then used school ID to join the records.
  3. I checked for missing serial numbers, invalid school IDs, condition ratings outside 1–5, negative costs, and exact duplicates.
  4. I split the import-ready rows from the rows that needed review. Then I built PivotTables and dashboards in Excel, repeated the analysis in Google Sheets, and wrote down the steps so I could run them again.
How I checked it

I counted the rows before and after

  • Improvements: 227 matched (96.19%), and 9 went to the exception list. The check found no repeated school and project pairs. The match rate tells me how many rows joined. I'd still review the individual matches before importing them.
  • Equipment: 1 exact duplicate, 9 valid records, and 3 exceptions accounted for all 13 rows. All 9 valid records made it through the join without creating extra rows.

I kept the rows that failed and wrote down why. If I were doing the import, that list is where I’d start.

What I found

Four assets drove most of the repair cost

The 9 valid assets averaged 2.89 out of 5 for condition and added up to $66,400 in simulated repairs. Four assets rated critical or poor made up $55,300, about 83% of the total. HVAC alone accounted for $38,900.

All 7 numbers matched. The saved Copilot workbook reported 14 of 15 quality checks passed. The one failure flagged 8 column headers that needed to be lowercase. This test started with clean data. I'd fix the headers and test a refresh with new rows before relying on it for a live import.

What I'd do next

What I’d check before an import

I'd go back to the original files for the rows I couldn't match, fix what I can verify, and run the checks again. I'd also hand off the list of anything I still couldn't resolve so the team knows what to look for.

I saved the manual Excel workbook, the Copilot comparison, the Google Sheets analysis, and a written procedure for rerunning the workflow.
