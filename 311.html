Columbus 311: What Do Residents Ask the City For, and How Fast Does It Get Done?

Why I did this

It's my city

Every pothole, missed trash pickup, and broken streetlight in Columbus gets logged as a 311 request. I'm from Columbus, so I wanted to see what people actually complain about and how fast the city fixes it.

The data

What I pulled

  • 310,857 records: every 311 request reported in Columbus in 2025, pulled from the city's public ArcGIS REST service, 2,000 records per call
  • 11 fields kept: case ID, status, reported date, close date, department, division, category, request type, ZIP, community, and council district
How I did it

Step by step

1. Look at it first

Before changing anything, I looked at every column for blanks, repeats, and weird statuses. 26% of rows had no council district, 3% had no close date, and 13,672 case IDs showed up more than once.

2. The duplicates weren't really duplicates

Those 13,672 repeats looked like duplicates, but most were one case with several request types. Only 955 were true duplicates (same case and same request type). If I'd just deleted every repeated case ID, I would've thrown out about 12,700 real requests.

3. Clean it (pandas)

  • Removed 955 true duplicates
  • Removed 100,325 internal staff notes. They aren't resident requests, and they close in seconds, which would mess up the timing
  • Switched the times from UTC to Eastern, so New Year's Eve requests stopped showing up as January 2026
  • Flagged 6,451 requests marked Closed with no close date and kept them out of timing
  • Filled blank labels with "Unknown"

4. Double-check it

I ran 5 checks: no duplicate case/request pairs, row counts reconcile, nothing closed before it was reported, every timed request has a close date, and every request falls in 2025. The first run failed 3 of 5. That's how I caught the time zone and missing-date problems. After the fixes, all 5 passed.

5. Put it in SQL

Built a star schema in SQLite (a 209,577-row request fact table joined to a 468-row request type table) and answered 6 questions with JOINs, GROUP BY, HAVING, a CTE, and LAG for month-over-month change.

6. Get it ready for Power BI

Exported the tables for a Power BI dashboard, plus a data dictionary that explains every field, rule, and metric.

What I found

The answers

  • 209,577 resident requests in 2025 after cleaning
  • Trash, recycling, and yard waste = 49% of everything. The #1 request: "90-gallon container needs serviced" (15,992)
  • Volume peaked in July (21,714) and fell 24.8% from October to November
  • Citywide, requests closed in 14.0 days on average (median 4.2), and 61.4% closed within a week
  • Building and Zoning was slowest (25.2 days, 44.6% within a week). Neighborhoods was fastest (4.9 days, 89.3%)
  • District 7 generated the most requests (48,846)
So what

What I'd tell the city

Half of all requests are trash and recycling, so that's where fixes would help the most. Building and Zoning closes less than half its requests within a week, so I'd look at their staffing first. And if I hadn't caught the fake duplicates and the time zone bug, the counts would've been off by thousands.
