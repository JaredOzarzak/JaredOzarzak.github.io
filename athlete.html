Why I did this

The question

When I was a sports scientist at Ohio Athletic Performance, coaches needed numbers fast. There are a few key things they want to know: is this kid strong, fast, or explosive? What's their RSI-mod? What should we work on? So I built a dashboard where you can pick one athlete and get your answer in a few seconds.

The data

What I worked with

  • A team of 30 athletes with 11 tests each: body mass, bench, squat, IMTP peak force, vertical jump, broad jump, CMJ height, CMJ RSI-mod, 20-yard dash, 40-yard dash, and the 5-10-5 shuttle. You can always add whatever tests you want
  • The first 6 come from a practice dataset from a data analytics course. I simulated the rest, plus positions (Skill, Combo, Line), based on each athlete's original results. Names are made up so it reads like a real roster
How I did it

Step by step

1. Put every test on the same scale

You can't compare 2,450 N to 23 inches to 4.8 seconds. So I turned every test into a T-score: 50 is the team average and every 10 points is one standard deviation. I flipped the sprints and shuttle so a faster time gives a higher score.

2. Make strength fair

I scored bench, squat, and IMTP relative to body weight, so a bigger kid doesn't win just for being bigger. Body mass and the raw lifts are still there for context.

3. Group the tests into 4 categories

Strength (relative bench, squat, IMTP), Explosiveness (vertical, broad, CMJ height, RSI-mod), Speed (20-yard and 40-yard), and Change of Direction (5-10-5). Each category is the average of its tests, so a category with more tests doesn't count more.

4. Roll it into one number

The TSA (Total Score of Athleticism) is the average of the 4 categories, so 50 is the team average. They're weighted equally for now, and you could adjust the weights by sport or position. Missing tests are skipped, never counted as zero.

5. Build the lookup and team view

Used INDEX and MATCH so one dropdown drives everything: results, team and position averages, T-scores, percentiles, ranks, and a rating from "Needs Work" to "Elite." The team tab ranks everyone, color-codes strengths and weaknesses, and compares positions.

What I found

The results

  • Cole Patterson was the best overall athlete at 67.0 TSA, top 3 on the team in all 4 categories. Click on him up top and see it for yourself
  • TSA ranged from 35.1 to 67.0, and 14 of 30 athletes were above the team average
  • Andre Coleman was 2nd on the team in explosiveness but 4th overall, because he was closer to the middle of the pack in relative strength, speed, and change of direction. One great category doesn't make you the best overall athlete
  • The radar chart shows where an athlete is lopsided, like explosive but slow to change direction, which is what a coach needs to plan training
Short version

What I'd tell you in 30 seconds

I turned 11 different tests into one scale across 4 categories, then built a one-click lookup in Excel that shows an athlete's strengths, weaknesses, and where they rank on the team and at their position.

What I'd add next

Tracking fatigue

This is a one-time test, so it can't show fatigue. With real force plate data tested every week, I'd track CMJ height and RSI-mod over time. A drop from an athlete's own baseline is the flag that tells a coach to adjust training.
