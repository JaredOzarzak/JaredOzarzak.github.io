Why I did this

The question

When I was a sports scientist at Ohio Athletic Performance, coaches didn't want a spreadsheet full of numbers. They wanted to know: is this kid strong, fast, or explosive compared to the rest of the team, and what should we work on? So I built a dashboard where you pick one athlete and get the answer in a few seconds.

The data

What I worked with

  • 30 athletes with 7 tests each: body mass, bench 1RM, squat 1RM, IMTP peak force, vertical jump, broad jump, and sprint time
  • A practice dataset from a data analytics course, with made-up names so it reads like a real roster
How I did it

Step by step

1. Put every test on the same scale

You can't compare 2,450 N to 23 inches to 2.06 seconds. So I turned every test into a T-score: 50 is the team average and every 10 points is one standard deviation. I flipped sprint so a faster time gives a higher score.

2. Roll it into one number

I added up the 6 performance T-scores into a TSA (Total Score of Athleticism), so the team average is about 300. I left body mass out of the total since being heavier isn't "better." It's just there for context.

3. Build the lookup

Used INDEX/MATCH so one dropdown drives everything: the result, team average, T-score, rank out of 30, and a rating from "Needs Work" to "Elite." Conditional formatting turns scores green or red so you can scan it fast.

4. Add the team view

A second tab ranks all 30 athletes by TSA with a leaderboard chart, plus how far each one is above or below the team average.

What I found

The results

  • Andre Coleman was the best overall athlete at 401 TSA, more than 100 points above the team average
  • TSA ranged from 216 to 401, so there's a big gap between the top and bottom of the roster
  • 15 of 30 athletes were above the team average
  • The spider chart shows where an athlete is lopsided, like strong in the weight room but slow off the line, which is exactly what a coach needs to plan training
Short version

What I'd tell you in 30 seconds

I turned 7 different tests into one scale so you can compare apples to apples, then built a one-click lookup in Excel that shows any athlete's strengths, weaknesses, and team rank.
