Hospital Visits by Specialty: Which Ones Keep Hospitals Busiest?

Short answer: Internal Medicine had the most hospital visits (14,635) and the most total hospital days (67,084). But its stays weren't long, about 4.6 days each. It led because of volume. The longest stays were in smaller specialties like Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, at 8.9 days per visit.
Why I did this

Most visits isn't the same as most busy

I wanted to see which specialties had the most hospital visits. But a lot of visits doesn't automatically mean a lot of time in the hospital. One specialty might have tons of short stays, and another might have fewer visits that last a lot longer. So I looked at it three ways: how many visits, how long the average stay was, and how many total hospital days added up across all those visits.

The data

What I worked with

  • Table: patient.health in MySQL Workbench
  • One row = one hospital visit (an encounter), not one patient. The same patient can show up more than once, so these are visit counts, not patient counts
  • Unknown specialties: some rows had a ? instead of a specialty. That means it wasn't recorded, so I left those rows out instead of guessing
How I measured it

Three numbers, three questions

  • Visits: how many hospital encounters each specialty had. I just counted the rows
  • Average stay: the typical number of days a visit lasted for that specialty
  • Total days: every day of every stay added up. For example, three stays of 2, 3, and 5 days add up to 10 hospital days

Total days is the one that shows where the hospital's time and beds actually go, because it combines how often patients come in with how long they stay.

1. Which 10 specialties had the most hospital visits?

I counted visits for each specialty, left out the unknown ones, and ranked them from most to least.

Show the SQL 
SELECT medical_specialty,
       COUNT(*) AS visits
FROM patient.health
WHERE medical_specialty <> '?'
GROUP BY medical_specialty
ORDER BY visits DESC
LIMIT 10;

In plain English: count the visits for each specialty, skip the unknowns, and show the top 10.

2. Which 10 specialties had the longest average hospital stays?

This one looks at how long a typical visit lasts, not how many visits there are. A specialty can have fewer patients but keep them in the hospital longer.

The catch I ran into: my first version was led by specialties with fewer than 50 visits. A couple of long stays can make an average look huge. So I added a rule: a specialty needs at least 50 visits to make the list.

Show the SQL 
SELECT medical_specialty,
       COUNT(*) AS visits,
       ROUND(AVG(time_in_hospital), 1) AS avg_stay_days
FROM patient.health
WHERE medical_specialty <> '?'
GROUP BY medical_specialty
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 50
ORDER BY avg_stay_days DESC
LIMIT 10;

In plain English: find the average days per visit for each specialty, only keep specialties with at least 50 visits, then show the 10 longest.

3. Which specialties accounted for the most total hospital days?

This adds up every day of every stay for each specialty. It puts visits and length of stay together, so it shows which specialties use the most hospital time overall.

Show the SQL 
SELECT medical_specialty,
       SUM(time_in_hospital) AS total_days
FROM patient.health
WHERE medical_specialty <> '?'
GROUP BY medical_specialty
ORDER BY total_days DESC
LIMIT 10;

In plain English: add up all the hospital days for each specialty and rank them from most to least.
